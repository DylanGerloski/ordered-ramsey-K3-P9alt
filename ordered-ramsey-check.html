What is being checked

Take the complete graph on vertices 0, 1, …, n−1, kept in that order, and give every edge one of two colours. The ordered Ramsey number R_ord(K_a, P_b^alt) is the smallest n at which every such colouring must contain a clique K_a in the first colour or an ordered copy of the alternating path P_b^alt in the second.

So a colouring of K_n with neither one proves R_ord > n. The check is finite: every set of a vertices for the clique, and every set of b vertices for the path.

The alternating path P_7^alt

On b ordered vertices the path runs first, last, second, second to last, and so on inward. An ordered copy keeps the left-to-right order of the vertices it lands on.

What this does and does not show

  • K_4 vs P_7^alt. Table 15 of the paper prints this cell as at least 22, and its Conjecture 4.22 gives 23. The colouring of K_22 here shows at least 23. It is a lower bound only. I have not shown the upper bound.
  • K_3 vs P_9^alt. The colouring of K_19 here is the lower half of the value 20, the cell Table 9 leaves as at least 20. The upper half is a proof that no colouring of K_20 exists, split into 256 cases, each with a machine-checked proof. A browser cannot re-run that part. The material to re-run it is in the public repository, about 6 core-hours in all.
  • Limits. Every one of those 256 proofs rests on the same build of one proof checker, and nobody outside my own lab has re-run them yet. This page checks the colourings and nothing more.
